## What was hard about this pass

### 1. Every structural gate passed, and the vocabulary was guessed

The first-hour gate was clean and stayed clean: the Name **152 of 153**
seats reading Yahweh, ⟨את⟩ **700 seats with none fabricated** (one exception
below), no Tagalog or Cebuano in the flow, no Latin where it should not be,
Genesis 1:1 exactly as the rails wrote it, and the number verses correct by
eye (Gen 5:5 = 930 as *siam a gasut a tawen ken tallopulo a tawen*).

Underneath that, words the rails had not named were being invented — *naked*
came out three different ways in six verses, *to pour* as a word that means
nothing, *strife* as a word that means **calamity**, *ashamed* as a word that
means **afraid**.

**A rendering can be structurally perfect and lexically invented, and every
gate we own will say it passed.** That is this chair's finding, and it is why
the lexicon audit below ships beside these notes.

### 2. The rails fix only the words the rails name

A vocabulary table was added at lighting — the Bambara lesson applied forward
rather than after a failure. It worked exactly where it reached: *lawag ·
dara · daga · langit · aldaw · kayo · bulong* all came back right. And the
invention simply moved to the words the table did not carry.

The table was widened four more times during the burn (the discipline caches
must be cleared on both sides or the fire keeps reading the old file). Each
widening fixed its own words and no others.

### 3. The model's competence has a shape, and it falls off at the register

Words were certified by a round trip — English → Ilocano, then the answers
shuffled with no English in sight → English, keeping only what came home.
**121 of 224 concepts**, and the shape matters more than the total:

| grain | certified |
|---|---|
| body parts, states, everyday verbs | 29 / 36 |
| household, land, sky, animals | 19 / 30 · 16 / 21 |
| **mercy · truth · covenant · sacrifice · to anoint · oath · pillar** | **11 / 34, then 8 / 30 on retry** |

The register a Tanakh is written in is the register the model holds worst.
Asked for *foolishness* it returned its own deliberation in the answer field,
ending: **`too unreliable; answer anyway.`** That is not a quirk of the probe.
The renderer has no way to abstain either — every token must receive a gloss —
so that sentence is the standing condition of this work on any word the model
does not know.

### 4. The probe convicted the language

Words were added to the gate's watch list because the blind back-translation
answered *unknown*. Then the verses were read:

- **sanguanan** — לפני, Gen 10:9, *"a mighty hunter **before** Yahweh."* Right.
- **ubbing** — הילדים plural, Gen 33:13, *"the children."* Right. The only
  fault was Gen 21:16, where the Hebrew is singular.
- **ruangan** — שער, Gen 28:17, *"the **gate** of heaven."* Right.
- **parangarang** — came home *display* once and *revealed* the next time;
  *revealed* is right for the niphal of ראה.

All four were struck. The back-translation asks what a word means **with no
sentence around it**, and *"I do not recognise this"* is not *"this is not a
word."* That is the Balochi `ar-letters` failure in new clothes — a probe
that convicted 3,716 true rows on chair 97 — and it was walked into again
here, the same night the warning was written into this chair's own roster
comment. **A probe hit is a verse to read, never a verse to convict.**

### 5. The trip is noisy

`tulang` came home as *bone* in one run and *sibling* in another. `linteg`
came home as *straight* in one batch and *lightning* in another. Same model,
same temperature; the shuffled list and the surrounding batch change the
answer. **One run is not a verdict.** Words are certified on agreement across
runs; that is how *agnaed* (to dwell) was seated after the forward call kept
dropping it.

### 6. A commandment came back inverted

Exodus 20:6 — וּלְשֹׁמְרֵי מִצְוֹתָי, *and to those who **keep** my
commandments* — came back as *kadagiti **agbaliwala** kadagiti bilin ko*, and
`agbaliwala` comes home from the blind trip as **disregard**, `baliwala` as
**neglect**. The chair said God shows hesed to those who *disregard* his
commandments.

Nothing structural could see it. The Name was right, ⟨את⟩ was right, `hesed`
was transliterated exactly as the rails ask, `agay-ayat` and `sangaribu` and
`lagipem` are all certified words standing correctly in the same passage. The
verse is clean by every gate we own and says the opposite of the Hebrew.

**And it points at a hole I left.** The certified table has *to remember* and
*to forget* — and not **to keep, to guard, to observe**, which is the covenant
verb of the whole Tanakh (Lev 8:35's own charge is *ושמרתם משמרת*). The table
was built outward from what a verse needs generally, and the word the
covenant turns on was never certified. That gap is now being filled first.

### 7. The model loops

Two flows came back with a word repeated: Gen 21:16 *"a mamati a mamati"*,
and Gen 41:40 *"umilong ti amin a dagiti umilong ti umilong a tattaok"* where
the Hebrew says *by your mouth all my people shall be ruled*. This costs no
vocabulary knowledge to detect in any language, and the probe belongs in the
fleet census, not only here.

### 8. One thing it got right that is worth recording

**שש is two words in Hebrew** — *six* and *fine linen* — and the chair told
them apart. Across the corpus: *innem* (six) 4 times where the text counts,
*lino* / *napino a lino* 6 times where the text weaves. Nothing in the rails
told it to do that. Whatever else is unreliable here, the disambiguation of a
homonym by context is working.

### 9. Leviticus 8:35 — the charge's own verse, and the loop closing

The Selah project is named from this verse: the centre of the Torah's 4D
space, *seven days, keep the charge of YHWH*. It landed at 01:20 and it
landed right:

> Ken iti ruangan ti Tolda ti Gimong agtugaw kayo iti aldaw ken iti rabii,
> iti pito nga aldaw, ket **salaknibennakayo ⟨את⟩ ti salaknib** ni Yahweh,
> ket saan kayo a matay, ta kastoy ti **bilin** kaniak.

Every load-bearing word in it is a word certified during this burn —
*salaknib · bilin · matay · pito · rabii* — with *Tolda ti Gimong* from the
rails' marked-words table. And the Hebrew's own figure, **ושמרתם משמרת**,
*keep the keeping*, verb and noun from one root, is reproduced exactly:
**salaknibennakayo ti salaknib**.

Three hours earlier this same chair inverted Exodus 20:6 and said God shows
hesed to those who *disregard* his commandments. That failure is what sent us
looking for the covenant verb at all; `salaknib` went into the rails on
two-run agreement because of it. It came back standing here.

That is the whole method in one verse: read by eye, find the fault, certify
the word twice, put it in the rails, clear the caches — and the fire renders
the charge correctly the next time it meets it.

### 10. "Let there be laughing"

`agtallok` was flagged at Genesis 1:3 in the first hour and put on the watch
list because the blind trip answered *unknown*. Under the session's own rule
that is only a reason to read, so it stayed a watch entry and not a
conviction — and for four hours I did not go back to it.

At gate 13 its count had grown to fourteen, so I looked at what it was
glossing. It is **היה** — *to be, to become, and it came to pass* — the most
frequent verb in the Hebrew Bible, roughly 3,500 occurrences:

> Genesis 1:3  יהי אור ויהי אור → *Agtallok ti lawag — ket nagtallok ti lawag*

And the second run of the trip brought `agtallok` home as **laughing**,
`nagtallok` as **laughed**. The chair was rendering *let there be light* as
**let there be laughing**.

The right words were there all along and came home 5 of 5, agreed twice:
**napasamak** (it came to pass) · **mapasamak** (to happen) · **agbalin /
nagbalin** (to become) · **adda** (to be, there is) · **adda koma** (let there
be). All are now in the rails.

**The lesson is about the watch list, not the word.** A watch entry is a
verse to read — and I left this one unread for four hours while it spread
across nineteen verses and counting. The rule was right; my follow-through
was not. **Watch entries need a count and a re-read each gate, and the ones
whose counts are GROWING need it first**, because a growing count means the
fault is still being produced.

### 11. The rate

~11 verses a minute against the ~52 of recent chairs, with the tier gate
reading `{:free 0 :waiting 29}` — saturated, not stalled. The rails doc grew
to 25 KB during the burn (hil.md is 13.5 KB) and the whole of it rides in
every system prompt.

### 11. The one question worth a reader's first hour — `lamolamo`

The machine uses **lamolamo** for **ערוה** (*nakedness*) in **twenty places**
across Leviticus 18 and 20, and it uses it *well*: `lamolamo` ·
`kinalamolamo` (the abstract) · `lamolamona` (his) · `ti lamolamo na`. That is
correct Ilocano morphology, not the flat repetition of an invented word.

The blind back-translation cannot bring it home. **Three times, "unknown."**
So by this chair's own rule it is uncertified, and it was struck from the
tables in the first hour for exactly that reason.

Three witnesses, and they disagree:

1. **The shovel proposed it** in the first hour, from training. That is not
   evidence; it is the same source that produced the errors.
2. **The machine produced it independently** twenty times, inflected
   correctly, after it had been removed from the rails.
3. **The probe does not know it**, three runs.

The probe is the weakest of the three — it asks what a word means with no
sentence around it, and it has already convicted four true Ilocano words
tonight. But it is the only one of the three that is not just a language
model talking to itself.

**So the question, and it is the highest-value one in this file: is
`lamolamo` the Ilocano for *naked*?** If yes, it belongs in the tables and
Genesis 2:25 and 3:7 must be re-pressed to use it. If no, **twenty verses of
Leviticus 18 and 20 are wrong.**

And either way ערוה currently has **six renderings** — *lamolamo ·
kinalamolamo · nakardaro · ti nakawan-bado · naulbaw · ti ilida*. Whichever
is right, it should be one.

### 12. A class the probe cannot settle — chair-consistent, trip-unstable

Two words now show the same shape, and it is worth naming because the
LEXICON-AUDIT will be full of it:

| word | the chair | the blind trip |
|---|---|---|
| `lamolamo` for ערוה | 20 uses, properly inflected | *unknown* ×3 |
| `nadalus` for טהור | 20 uses | *diligent* · *clean* · *swept* |
| `narugit` for טמא | 17 uses | *dirty* · *dirty* · *dirt* (one run) |

The machine uses these steadily and the probe cannot pin them. That is not
the same as invention — an invented word is used **once or twice and
inconsistently**, and it does not inflect. These inflect, they recur, and
they sit in the right semantic field every time the probe answers at all
(`dalus` lands on *sweeping* and *cleaning*; `rugit` on *dirt*).

So they are neither certified nor convicted. **They are the exact cases a
native ear settles in a minute and no amount of machine testing will.** They
are named in the rails' open table with their counts and their three
readings, and they belong at the top of `LEXICON-AUDIT.md` — above the words
that merely failed once, because these are the ones carrying whole chapters.

`nasantuan` for *holy* did certify twice and stands; the conflation of
**clean** with **holy** (`a nasantoan` ×5 for טהר, `di nasantuan` ×3 for טמא)
is a separate fault and a press seat, because Leviticus keeps קדש and טהר
distinct.

### 13. A homograph read the wrong way — מדבר

**מדבר** has **eight** renderings in this chair: *kaatapan* 5 · *desierto* 5 ·
*liwaliwan* 3 · **agsasarita** 2 · *daliaoan* 2 · *away* 2 · *kadkaan* 2 ·
*iliw* 1.

Two of them are not a synonym problem at all. **`agsasarita` means
CONVERSING.** Unpointed מדבר is either *midbar*, the wilderness, or
*medabber*, the piel participle of דבר, *speaking* — and the chair took the
second reading twice. `kadkaan` comes home as *mockery* and `iliw` as
*longing*; neither is a wilderness.

Worth setting beside §8: the chair tells **שש** apart as *six* and *fine
linen* with nothing in the rails to help it. So homographs here are a
coin-flip rather than a blind spot — which is harder to guard against than a
consistent failure, because nothing in the output marks which way it went.

A press seat, and one more argument for the single-rendering rule: whichever
Ilocano word is right for *midbar*, there should be one.

### 14. The sacrifice system collapses, and three more homographs

A collapse detector — for every Ilocano content word, how many distinct
Hebrew surfaces land on it — found the two that matter most.

**The offerings.** Leviticus distinguishes them absolutely; this chair does
not:

| Hebrew | what it is | rendered |
|---|---|---|
| קרבן | offering, general | **daton** ×25, consistent |
| עלה | burnt offering | *sumka* · *natotong a sakripisio* · *natuno a sakripisio* |

`daton` alone carries **77 distinct Hebrew surfaces**. The system that the

**And three more homographs, all found in this one tally:**

- **עלה** — *olah*, the burnt offering, read as *alah*, **he went up**
  (`sumka`)
- **אשה** — *ishsheh*, the fire offering, read as *ishshah*, **woman**
  (`babai` ×20)
- **עז** — *ez*, a goat, read as *oz*, **strength** (`napigsa` · `bileg`)

With מדבר from §13 that is **four**. The pattern is not random and it is not
frequency alone: the chair resolves an unpointed form without using the
sentence around it.

**And this one is mechanically checkable in any chair.** The graph holds the
lemma and Strong's number for every token. A census could flag *gloss
inconsistent with lemma* — עלה glossed as motion where the lemma is H5930,
אשה glossed as a person where the lemma is H801 — with no knowledge of the
target language at all. That would catch a fault class no probe here has
caught, in every chair at once.

**The flock collapses too**: כבש lamb, צאן flock, איל ram all → `karnero`,
though the chair writes **urbon a karnero** (*young of the sheep*) for כבש
five times — again its own better instinct, under-used. עז is correctly
`kalding` where it is not misread as strength.

## §15 — The thigh has no word here

The bleed probe flagged `paa` eight times. Reading the verses turned it into
something larger than a Tagalog loan.

`paa` stands for שוק, the **thigh of the heave offering** (Exo 29:22,27;
Lev 7:32,33,34; Num 6:20) and for ירך, **thigh** (Gen 32:26 — the hollow of
Jacob's thigh; Num 5:22 — the curse that makes the thigh rot). In Tagalog
`paa` is *foot*. In Ilocano *foot/leg* is **saka** and *thigh* is **luppo**.

Then the tally, over the whole corpus so far:

| Hebrew | how many | how the chair renders it |
|---|---:|---|
| שוק *thigh (heave)* | 16 | `ti paa` ×5 · `saka` ×3 · then *ti kayatmo* (**your desire**) · *naabuso* · *pinigsaan* · *ken dagiti bendada* … |
| ירך *thigh* | 20 | `bangir` (side) ×4 · `apitmo` (**your harvest**) ×3 · `piego` ×2 · `ti paa` ×2 · *ti puingan* · *yao* · *imbagannaka* · *a malaka* … |

Ten different glosses for one body part in twenty occurrences, and several of
them are not body parts at all. **This is not one wrong word; it is a register
that collapsed.** The repair pattern we have used four times tonight — tally
the term, find the chair already doing it right part of the time, pin its own
better instinct — has nothing to pin here. The chair has no instinct for the
thigh. It goes to a native ear, and `luppo` is our guess, not our finding.

Contrast the sheep, where the same tally *does* resolve: צאן → **karnero** 44
of 79, כבש → **karnero** in nearly every hit, and `tupa` twice. There the
chair knows and slips. Here it does not know.

**And the same tally showed a third thing: the peoples' names leak Spanish.**

| Hebrew | spellings | what stands |
|---|---:|---|
| יעקב | 5 | Yaakov ×130 · Yakov ×11 · Yakop ×3 · Yaakob ×2 · Yaaqob ×2 |
| רחל | 4 | Rahel · Rachel · Rakhel · Rakel |
| האמרי *Amorite* | 8 | Emori ×10 · Amorita ×8 · **Amorreo** ×3 · Amorrei ×2 · Amoriteo ×2 · Amorreh ×2 |
| היבוסי *Jebusite* | 6 | Yebusi ×5 · Yebusiano · Yebuseo · **Jebuseo** · Yevusi · Yebusita |
| הכנעני *Canaanite* | 9 | Kenaanan ×7 · Kenaano ×3 · Kenaani ×3 · Kanaan · Kenani · Kenaan · Kenaanita |

The patriarchs hold: 130 of 148 on one form. The **gentilics** scatter — and
they scatter into **Spanish** (*Amorreo*, *Jebuseo*), which is where the
Ilocano Bible tradition came from. The chair is reaching for the form its
training has seen in Ilocano print, and that form is Spanish. Pinnable in the
rails: one transliteration per nation.

## §16 — The unpointed את is two words, and the gate convicted the wrong one

Gate 20 reported `את lost 9`. All nine were false.

The object marker is pointed **אֵת / אֶת** (tsere or segol) — Strong's H853.
The second-person feminine singular pronoun ***thou*** is pointed **אַתְּ /
אָתְּ** (patach or qamats, tav with shva) — H859. Strip the points and they are
the same three letters, and this corpus stores unpointed surfaces.

So the gate demanded a ⟨את⟩ marker on *thou*:

- Gen 12:11 — כִּֽי־אִשָּׁ֥ה יְפַת־מַרְאֶ֖ה **אָֽתְּ** · *thou art a fair woman*
- Gen 24:60 — אֲחֹתֵ֕נוּ **אַ֖תְּ** · *thou art our sister*
- Num 11:15 — וְאִם־כָּ֣כָה **אַ֣תְּ** עֹֽשֶׂה־לִּ֗י · *if thou deal thus with me*

`sika` (*you*) is the right answer in every one of them; a marker would have
been the wrong one. Fifty verses in the whole Tanakh carry this collision, 53
tokens, derived from the pointed OSHB by the vowel rule and now baked into the
gate as `+thou-verses+`.

With them excluded the chair's marker record is **3,299 seats, 0 lost**. Not
one object marker dropped.

That is the **ninth** probe hit tonight that turned out innocent when the verse

## §19 — A tally by letters cannot see a Hebrew verb

Three words were pinned at 05:05 (§17). The obvious next move was to measure
them the way היה and וידבר were measured — split the corpus at the pin, count
before and after. It did not work, and the two reasons are both worth keeping.

### A pin's proof has a due time

146 verses landed in the first ten minutes after the pin and contained exactly
**one** occurrence of the three pinned terms. The graph says what remains ahead
of the burn: **79 מנחה · 144 איב · 207 נפל** across 17,300 verses. At the
observed 11.5 verses a minute that is one hit every **19 · 10.5 · 7.3**
minutes, so ten hits — enough to read a shift — are due at roughly **08:30 ·
07:00 · 07:00**.

היה and וידבר were provable within half an hour only because they occur in the
thousands. **Checking a sparse pin before its proof is due is not diligence, it
is noise.** Write the due time down and wait for it.

### And the count was wrong anyway, in a way particular to Hebrew

Joshua 11:7 landed at 05:13, after the pin, and renders **ויפלו** — *and they
fell upon them* — as **`natnagda`**. The pinned word, in service, plain on the
page. And the tally reported נפל AFTER = **0**.

Because the tally is a **substring match on the surface**, and **Hebrew does
not keep its root intact**. The nun of נפל assimilates in the imperfect:
**ויפלו · יפל · תפל** have no nun in them at all. A search for the three
letters finds only the forms that happen to keep them.

So:

- the "35 hits" figure for נפל is **low**, and so is every other **verb** tally
  taken by substring tonight;
- the **noun** tallies are sound — איב · מנחה · צאן · שוק · ירך keep their
  letters, which is why those results held up;
- **the verb pins can only be measured by lemma, not by letters.**

That is the gloss-against-lemma tool, already filed and waiting. It belongs to
the same discipline as the lemma-id rule: *read the letters* — and know when
the letters are not there to be read.

Joshua 11:3 lists all seven nations in clean, consistent Hebrew forms —
*Kenaani · Amori · Khiti · Perizzi · Yevusi · Khivi*, not one Spanish form.
That looked like evidence the gentilic scatter of §15 was **positional**: an
early drift the chair had since settled out of.

The by-book split refutes it. **Joshua still scatters** — *Emori* ×6, *Amori*
×2 and **Amorrei** ×2 for one nation; *Yevusi*, *Yebusita* and **Hebusio** for

clean it reads.

### A third notation leak

Joshua 11:3's flow opens `ti Kenaani **a(n)ga** manipud iti daya`. The chair
could not choose between `a` and `nga` and **shipped both, in parentheses,

## §23 — The first pin that did not take, and why that was worth more than the pin

### Reading the live prompt before rewriting anything

The temptation is to assume the edit did not land — a stale cache, an

A flat surface rule with nothing to decide.

## §31 — The foreign script is a decoding-level fault, and the rails cannot reach it

Ilocano only, no Han, no Cyrillic, no Greek, no Arabic outside ⟨ ⟩; no `∅`, no
`~`, no parenthesised alternates, no spoken self-correction; the supplied-word
mark is ⟨ ⟩ and never ASCII `<>`; a verse never stops in an ellipsis.

**It did not work.**

| when | verses holding a foreign script |
|---|---:|
| 05:12 | 16 |
| 08:16 | 24 |
| 08:36 | 24 |
| **08:58, thirty-two minutes after the pin** | **27** |

mechanical to describe, visible to any reader the moment the page is open. It

## §33 — Two ways a probe reports good news, both found by auditing the tool

Neither of these was found by a fault surfacing. Both were found by looking at
the instrument.

### A capped list is not a count

`cap` stops every sample list at twelve. Five printed counts were
`(count capped-list)`, which becomes the ceiling the moment a fault passes
twelve. **NON-LATIN printed 12 while the truth went 16 → 27. LOOPS printed 12
while the truth was 720.**

**And the two bugs were protecting each other.** The ceiling made the artifact
look small; the artifact would have made the corpus look broken the moment the
ceiling was lifted. Fixing either one alone would have produced a false alarm.

`cap` now keeps a true total beside every list, and the sample size prints
separately.

### A check scoped to the wrong population reports zero

`notation-leak` was computed only over the verses `scan` returned — verses that
already held a foreign character. So the parenthesised-alternate check written
for **Joshua 11:3** could never fire: that verse is pure Latin and its fault is
`a(n)ga`, not a script. A whole-corpus `leaks` sweep now runs it, and it
immediately found **2 Samuel 17:1** — Ahithophel's *twelve thousand* rendered
`ti dua sangapulo ket dua — saan: ti sangapulo ket dua a ribu`, *two ten and two
— **no**: ten and two thousand*. The chair began "twelve", saw the verse wants
twelve **thousand**, said *saan* out loud and corrected itself inside the text.

**A zero from a wrongly-scoped check looks exactly like a clean result.**

## §47 — Consistency is a separate property from correctness

## §59 — The latency of a pin, and why a timestamp is evidence

A pin does not take effect when the cache is cleared. The `איש` pin went into the
rails at **16:41:49** with both discipline caches cleared in the same call, and the
next three `איש` in Psalms still came out `ti tao` — **16:43:54 · 16:45:08 ·
16:48:03** — while every one from **16:49** onward is correct. Strictly
chronological.

**So a pin has a latency of roughly six to seven minutes, or eighty to a hundred
verses.** Calls already in flight and calls already prompted carry the old rails.

That reframed every *"the pin did not take"* reading of the day, and **I tested it
on the one I most wanted it to rescue and it failed**: the four wrong `mamati`
landed at 10:33, 11:20, 12:11 and 12:28, one to three **hours** after the 09:29
pin. Not one is inside the window. **The verdict stood. A correction that would be
convenient is exactly the one to test hardest.**

But the law's real value turned out to be something else, and it paid twice on
independent cases:

- **`kinakinalintegna`** — a doubled `kina` prefix, a fresh variant of the very
  fault the `kinalinteg` pin forbade. It looked like defiance. It landed **twenty-
  seven seconds before the pin.**
- **`ken tallopulo ket siam`** for ninety at genesis/5/17 — thirty-nine written for
  ninety in Mahalalel's 895 years. It looked like the number table failing. It
  landed **ninety-one seconds after an engine relight**, inside the window, and
  ninety is otherwise **19 of 20 correct**.

**Both times I had the fault written up as a failure before I looked.** The lesson
is not that pins are slow. It is that **a verse's timestamp is evidence and a
verse's content alone is not.**

**The `ñ` rule came out.** An acute is decoration on a correct word and strips

## §68 — The second §9 pass, and five more caught by reading

**The fifteen empty-gloss fills were dropped entirely.** The floor named every sense
so they looked deterministic; **reading the NEIGHBOURING tokens killed it:**

    2-samuel/21/7  מפי  → Mefiboshet (the whole name)          בשת  → ""

**An empty gloss is usually the second half of something rendered whole on the first
token**, and filling it writes *twelve ten*, *sixteen ten*, *Mefiboshet boshet* —
taking a correct rendering and making it wrong, **which is the exact class §9's
number rules exist to prevent.** The right test is not *is the gloss empty?* but
**is the SENSE missing from the verse?**, and that needs the neighbours.

**The A8 flow rule was firing on its string alone, under a comment claiming that was
safe.** The comment read *"the flow carries no Hebrew, so a token-pair rule fires on

anything.** It cost two regressions — **`joshua/20/3` and `20/5` read *without
KNOWLEDGE*, Hebrew `דָעַת`, correctly `pannakaammo`, and the flow rule rewrote them

divergence, from the pass whose first stated rule is that the flow must be repaired

**`2-chronicles/35/22` could not be a bare strip** — its row gloss for `אל` is the
